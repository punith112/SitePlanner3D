1. Getting Started

SitePlanner 3D is a single-page planning tool: you sketch a floor plan in 2D on the left, and the building grows in realtime 3D on the right. Everything runs in the browser — walls, doors, HVAC ducts, exhaust hoods, drains, electrical points, dimensions and clash checks — and the finished model exports to standard CAD formats (.STEP, .IGS, .OBJ) with one click.

No installation is needed. Open the tool, and you are greeted by the split screen shown in Figure 1. A good first exercise is:

1. Click Draw Walls and click four corners of a room in the floor plan.
2. Press Esc (or double-click) to finish.
3. Orbit the 3D view with the middle mouse button (SolidWorks style — see the 3D VIEW row below) or the left mouse button.
4. Add a door, a window and a pillar — each appears instantly in 3D.

3. Drawing Walls — AutoCAD Style

Select Draw Walls (or L.Wall for low walls such as kitchen counters and parapets), then work exactly like the AutoCAD LINE command:

1. Click the start point once. Do not hold the button.
2. Move the mouse — a rubber-band line follows the cursor showing the live length and angle in degrees, with an angle arc at the start point (Figure 2).
3. Click to place the point. Drawing chains — the next segment starts from the last point.
4. Double-click or press Esc to stop.

3.1 Dynamic input (type exact values)

While drawing, two small input boxes float next to the cursor — length (m) and angle (°). Type an exact value in either box, press Tab to jump between them, and press Enter to place the point precisely (e.g. length 2.5, angle 90 draws a 2.5 m wall straight up). A typed angle always wins over snapping.

3.2 Ortho snap — on/off (F8)

The ⊞ Ortho button (highlighted when ON) works like AutoCAD's ORTHO mode:

Ortho modes
Ortho ON (default)Ortho OFF
Walls lock strictly horizontal / vertical — perfect for clean rectangular plans. Even a diagonal drag produces a straight axis wall with the full drag length (Figure 3). Walls draw at any angle. Directions close to a 15° step (0°, 15°, 30°, 45°…) snap politely, so angled rooms are still easy to keep tidy.

Press F8 or click the button to flip modes at any time, even mid-wall.

3.3 Wall height — per wall

Wall H in the toolbar sets the floor's full wall height. A wall drawn with the L.Wall tool uses its own height (default 1.2 m). To give each wall its own height — for example one at 1.5 m, the next at 2.0 m, another at 1.8 m — double-click the wall (in Select or Move mode) and type the height; the 3D model updates immediately, and walls whose height differs from the floor height carry a small teal H badge in the plan (e.g. H1.5). The wall editor also sets thickness, or deletes the wall.

3.4 Snaps and grips (AutoCAD style)

While drawing, the cursor snaps to wall endpoints (green square marker) and midpoints (green triangle marker) — like AutoCAD's OSNAP. After drawing, select a single wall with the Select tool: blue grip squares appear at its ends and middle. Drag an end grip to stretch the wall (with snapping), or the middle grip to move it — exactly like AutoCAD grip editing.

4. Doors, Windows, Beams and Pillars

Set the size in the hint bar, then click a wall to insert:

To move any of them, use ✥ Move and drag. To resize a beam or pillar, click it in Move mode — a small editor opens, including its height from floor.

5. Floors and Slabs

Click ＋ Floor to add a storey. The floor selector jumps between floors; everything you draw belongs to the active floor, and floor heights stack automatically. Use the ▭ Slab tool and click two opposite corners to pour a concrete slab/terrace on top of the active floor.

6. The Items Library

＋ Add Items opens the equipment drawer (Figure 4) with categories for Hoods, Ducting, HVAC, Services, Blowers, Structures and Kitchen Equipment — exhaust hoods, fresh-air grilles in the three standard sizes, diffusers, AHUs, FCUs, drain points, switches and more.

Pick an item, adjust its dimensions in the dialog, then place it. Wall-mounted items (hoods, switches) ask you to click a wall — the side you click is the side they mount on. Floor items stamp with each click; press R to rotate, Esc to finish.

7. HVAC Ducting

The Duct tool draws duct runs with automatic fittings (Figure 5):

1. Choose the system in the hint bar — green = Fresh Air, orange = Exhaust.
2. Set width, height and the bottom elevation from the floor.
3. Click points along the run. Corners get mitered elbows automatically.
4. Double-click or Esc ends the run.

Change a segment's size later (Move mode → click the duct) and reducers are inserted automatically where sizes change.

7.1 Rises and vertical ducts

Use ⇧ Rise to take a duct up to the next floor with a top offset — the run continues from the offset point. Use ⬍ V.Duct to drop a vertical duct (e.g. from a terrace AHU down the facade) at any clicked point, inside or outside the building; it wears a weather cap on top.

7.2 Exhaust hoods

The library's wall-type hoods (trapezoid cross-section, grease filter at 45°, definable length and mounting height) mount exactly like doors — click a wall. Flip the side with the editor's ⇄ button.

8. Services — Drains and Electrical

In the library's Services category you will find gutter/floor-drain points (placed on the slab of the active floor) and wall-mounted electrical points — 16 A and 6 A switches, MCB boxes and motor starters — whose mounting height from the floor is editable (Move mode → click the item).

9. Measuring and Dimensioning

Three dedicated tools cover every measurement need:

9.1 2D Measure (floor plan only)

Click a first point, move (live length and angle), click the second point — the dimension stays on the plan (Figure 6). Clicks snap to wall ends and corners, and further clicks chain measurements. Esc/Del clears them all.

9.2 3D Measure (3D view only)

SolidWorks-style entity measuring: click a face, edge or point (it highlights orange), then click or Ctrl+click a second one. The 3DMeasure panel opens right inside the 3D view showing Distance, ΔX, ΔY and ΔZ in millimetres (Figure 7), with an in-model dimension line. Works for every combination — point↔point, point↔edge, edge↔edge, point↔face, edge↔face and face↔face (angle shown when faces are not parallel). Esc clears instantly.

9.3 Dim — AutoCAD-style annotation dimensions

The 📐 Dim tool places permanent drafting dimensions on the plan. Pick a mode in the hint bar:

Dim modes
ModeHow to use
LinearClick point 1 → point 2 → pull out and click to position. Pull up/down for a horizontal dim, sideways for vertical.
AlignedSame flow for inclined walls — the dimension line runs parallel to the points with the true inclined length.
ContinueEach click extends a string of aligned dims from the previous one.
BaselineEach click measures from the first origin of the last dim, stacked outward.
AngularClick two walls, then position the arc — the true angle appears in degrees.

Dimensions are permanent objects: they are saved with the project, undoable, and erasable one-by-one with the ✕ Delete tool.

10. Heights and the 3D Dims Layer

Every object can be moved vertically, not just in plan: ducts have an elevation, hoods and switches a mounting height, beams and pillars a height from floor — all editable from their Move-mode editors or the ↕ field in the selection bar. Press 📐 3D Dims to toggle a dimension layer in the 3D view itself (wall lengths, duct sizes, hood elevations), so heights and sizes can be read off the model directly.

11. Face Painting and Clash Detection

🎨 Paint: click any face in the 3D view and choose a colour from the palette — ideal for marking zones or equipment. Colours persist with the project; Clear restores the original.

⚠ Clash: runs a clash analysis of the ductwork against pillars, columns, beams, rises and other ducts, and warns when fresh-air intakes sit too close to exhaust zones. Hits are marked red (hard clash) or yellow (advisory) in both 2D and 3D, with a written report.

12. Saving and Exporting (.STEP / .IGS / .OBJ)

Click 💾 Save. Two things happen at once (Figure 10):

1. The project is saved in the browser — the 📂 Load button restores it next time, including all floors, ducts, dimensions and paint.
2. A download dialog opens offering the 3D model in three standard formats.

Export formats
FormatContentsBest for
.STEP (.stp)Solid B-rep geometry (AP203)SolidWorks, AutoCAD, Inventor, Revit — exact solid model
.IGS (.iges)Surface B-rep geometryOlder CAD systems and CAM pipelines
.OBJDisplay mesh (triangles)Blender, 3ds Max, rendering and visualisation tools

The toolbar's ⬇ .STEP and ⬇ .IGS buttons export the same files directly without opening the dialog. ⬆ Import reads DXF layouts and IGES/STEP/OBJ library models, and 🗑 New starts a fresh plan.

Tips for good drawings

· Keep Ortho ON for rectangular buildings; tap F8 only for angled walls.
· Read the hint bar — it always documents the active tool.

· Run ⚠ Clash after routing ducts, before exporting.
· Export .STEP for CAD work, .OBJ for rendering.

14. BIM and MEP Planning — Inspector, IFC Export, BOQ

SitePlanner 3D carries real BIM data behind the drawing, inspired by open-source BIM tools such as Bonsai BIM (IfcOpenShell): every object has an IFC class, a material and — for MEP work — a system and connection points. Three features work together:

14.1 BIM Inspector (🏷 BIM button)

Click 🏷 BIM in the toolbar, then click any object in the floor plan — a wall, door, window, beam, column, duct, hood, or any kitchen equipment. The inspector shows:

  • Live quantities — wall length / net area / volume (openings deducted), equipment sizes, duct lengths, mount heights.
  • IFC class — e.g. IfcWallStandardCase, IfcSanitaryTerminal for sinks, IfcFan for blowers, IfcElectricDistributionBoard for MCB panels.
  • Material — free text such as "AAC block 200mm" or "SS 304".
  • MEP system — Cold Water, Hot Water, Drainage, Gas, Electrical, Exhaust Air, Fresh Air.
  • Fire rating — on walls and doors.

Everything you enter is saved with the project and flows into the IFC export and the BOQ.

14.2 MEP connection points

Library items that need services carry connection points, drawn as coloured dots on the item in the plan and listed in the inspector and the equipment schedule:

MEP connection colour code
DotServiceTypical items
●Cold Watersinks, bain marie, FCU
●Hot Watersinks
●Drainagesinks, floor drains, bain marie, AHU / FCU
●Electricalgriddles, hoods, blowers, switches, MCB panels, starters
●Exhaust Airhoods, exhaust ducts and grilles, blowers
●Fresh Airfresh-air ducts, supply grilles, diffusers, AHU

14.3 IFC export (⬇ .IFC)

The ⬇ .IFC button (also in the Save dialog) downloads SitePlanner3D.ifc — an IFC2X3 BIM model with the full Project → Site → Building → Storey structure. Walls, slabs, doors, windows, beams, columns, kitchen equipment, ducts and services become native IFC elements with swept-solid geometry, and the materials you set in the inspector are attached to them. Open it in Bonsai BIM (Blender), Revit, ArchiCAD or any IFC viewer to coordinate with other disciplines.

14.4 BOQ / schedules (📑 BOQ)

The 📑 BOQ button downloads SitePlanner3D_Schedules.csv with five schedules, ready for Excel:

  • Wall schedule — per floor: IFC class, material, fire rating, length, height, thickness, gross/net area, volume, with totals.
  • Door & window schedule — sizes, sill heights, materials, fire ratings.
  • Kitchen equipment schedule — sizes, MEP connections, quantity per floor.
  • Duct schedule — system, segment sizes, elevation, length.
  • Electrical & services schedule — drains, switches, MCB panels, starters, mount heights.

15. Kitchen Layout Templates

The 🧩 Templates button opens a gallery of the five standard commercial-kitchen layouts. Pick one, set your room size, and the planner generates the complete starter design — enclosure walls, a door, and the full equipment set with BIM/MEP data already attached.

The five layout templates

Everything a template creates is a normal, editable object — stretch walls with the blue grips, double-click a wall to change its height, move, rotate or delete any equipment item, and adjust room size by dragging walls. Generating over an existing plan asks for confirmation and is one ↶ Undo step away. If none of the five fits, the dialog's bottom button closes it and you build custom with Draw Walls + Add Items.

16. BIM Data Points — One-Click Engineering Report

After designing — from a template or by hand — click ⚡ BIM Data. The planner computes the critical kitchen engineering data from the equipment you placed and presents it in six sections, each with a green ✓ / red ✗ verdict. ⬇ Download CSV exports the same data for Excel.

What the report checks
SectionOutput
1 · Electrical loadskW and amps (3-phase 400 V) per equipment; connected total to size the MCB feed
2 · Pipe sizesCold/hot water, gas and drain sizes per fixture
3 · Grease trapDrainage fixture count → required interceptor capacity vs installed; warns when a grease trap is missing (place one from the Services library)
4 · HVAC ventilationExhaust CMH per hood, fresh-air supply, 85% make-up-air rule, balance verdict, and exhaust-duct velocity check (6–12 m/s)

17. 3D Model Editing — SolidWorks-Style Direct Edit & V.Duct in 3D

Version 46 turns the 3D view from a viewer into an editor. Click 🧊 3D Edit in the toolbar: hovering any object in the 3D view highlights it yellow; clicking selects it orange and opens a floating edit panel with that object's live dimensions. Change a value, press Enter or ✔ Apply — the model and the 2D plan update together, and every edit is one ↶ Undo step. In this mode left-click selects/drags (like SolidWorks); orbiting stays on the middle mouse.

3D Edit operations

Click an objectSelects it (orange box) and opens the edit panel — equipment (L / W / H / mount / rotation), walls (length / height / thickness), pillars, beams, ducts (elevation & sizes), rises, vertical ducts (width / depth / bottom / top), wall hoods and service points
Click a blue dimension labelSelects the owning object and focuses the field that dimension displays — e.g. clicking a wall's length label lets you retype the wall length

Drawing a V.Duct in 3D: with the ⬍ V.Duct tool active you can now click directly in the 3D view — on any surface or open floor — and the vertical duct shaft drops through all floors at that exact spot, using the width, depth, bottom and top heights set in the top bar (Top = 0 runs it to the terrace). The same shaft can afterwards be resized or moved entirely in 3D with 🧊 3D Edit.

18. SolidWorks CommandManager — Features, Evaluate & View

Version 47 docks a CommandManager at the top of the 3D view, organised in the three SolidWorks-style tabs Features, Evaluate and View. Everything it creates or opens uses the standard SolidWorks names: Extruded Boss/Base, Edit Feature, Move/Copy Bodies, Measure, Mass Properties, Interference Detection, Zoom to Fit, standard Front / Top / Right / Trimetric orientations, Wireframe and the FeatureManager Design Tree; the edit panel itself is the PropertyManager.

The FeatureManager stays live: it refreshes automatically as you add, edit or delete bodies, on every floor. Combined with the PropertyManager (Section 17) this gives the full SolidWorks loop — create features, see them in the tree, click to edit their driving dimensions.
